The cancer-trained model does not predict base-editor off-targets.

A gradient-boosted model trained on APOBEC3-family mutations from tumour genomes predicts held-out tumour chromosomes at 5.369×. Applied to 216 million sites in base-edited HEK293T cells, it ranks the editor's actual off-target sites at 0.95–1.00× — flat — and below the deaminase-free control at every sharper threshold. The model works; the transfer does not.

Two results, in order: the architecture search that produced the strongest available model, and the transfer test that model then failed. The second is only meaningful because of the first.

Flat where it is well powered, depleted where it is sharp

At top 5% — 946 and 988 hits per arm — both clones sit at 0.954× and 1.001×. At the sharp tail the model actively avoids the editor's sites.

The gap runs the wrong way

The model ranks the deaminase-free control's sites above the editor's at every depth but the last. The control's mild 1.0–1.29× is the calling-bias signal it was included to expose.

The model leg has since been hardened twice

Both checks are on the model's cross-validated tail enrichment, not on the 5.369× in-sample figure above. Adding coverage decile to the trinucleotide+strand matching key left it intact (5.119× against 4.960× unmatched), and a leave-one-donor-out jackknife over all 21 donors gives ±0.30 — dropping any single donor moves the tail less than half the noise floor. What the model learned is neither a coverage artefact nor one person's mutations.

Not a coverage artefact — and the audit found the mechanism

The model's top sites have higher coverage than average (26.66 vs 25.98), and the depletion survives coverage banding in all four bands. But its top 0.1% is GC-rich (0.4562) while the editor's real off-targets are AT-shifted (0.354–0.378). The tumour-learned preference points at a different sequence regime than where this editor deposits damage.

03 — Reading itThree conclusions, and the control that separates them

The embeddings are empty, not diluted

Gradient boosting subsamples features and can ignore noise, yet 128 principal components of NT-v2 score 1.048× against a random baseline of 0.917×. Under an MLP it was 1.250×. Two heads, two architectures, same answer: genome-scale pretraining over 1 kb of context contributes nothing at the operating point.

The MLP's collapse was the head

Adding embeddings to structure cost the MLP almost everything (4.881 → 1.488) and cost gradient boosting essentially nothing (4.940 → 4.857). Six informative dimensions drown in 1,024 under a dense layer; trees simply don't select them. This control was queued before any of these numbers existed — without it, “embeddings hurt” and “embeddings are empty” are indistinguishable, and only one is true.

Nothing beats hairpin geometry

A convolutional motif scanner over ±200 bp — the right inductive bias for DNA, 2.5× the baseline's window — adds nothing to structure (4.810 vs 4.881) and reaches only 2.595× alone. Sequence is read better by a convolution than by a dense layer; sequence just isn't where the tail lives.

Calibration deserves the caveat it's usually given without: a constant predictor that always returns the base rate scores ECE 0.00000 and is useless. Low ECE alone proves nothing. What makes this model calibrated is that it moves — a 13.1× spread between its 0.1st and 99.9th prediction percentiles — and is right where it commits: at the top 0.1% it says 48.2% and the truth is 47.8%.

05 — SupportingWhat else the night settled

  • A model is required; a threshold isn't enough. The best single hand-written rule — trained on nothing, cross-validated against nothing, which if anything favours it — is hp_score at 2.988×. A stem ≥ 8 cut covers 2,229 sites at a 1.860× positive-rate ratio, but the top 0.1% needs 924, so every threshold must cut inside a tied block. Ranking within ties is exactly what the model supplies.
  • Site identity is not learnable, even in principle. Two disjoint halves of the donor cohort share 10 sites where chance predicts 4.6–17.6 depending on the assumed universe — at chance. 99.974% of sites appear in exactly one donor. The learnable target is a per-site propensity, never which site gets hit.
  • DNA parameters, not RNA. The thermodynamic block folds with Mathews 2004 DNA energies. On the same 150 windows, RNA parameters give a mean MFE of −21.1 kcal/mol against DNA's −8.9 — a 2.4× error that would have been invisible downstream.
  • AUROC was inverted against the tail at every step. Among the convolutional blocks, cnn_only has the best AUROC (0.5671) and roughly half the tail of struct_only, which has the worst (0.5388). Selecting on AUROC would have picked the weakest model at every decision.
  • Confounds cleared. GC in 10/10 deciles; sequence complexity in 10/10 on two independent measures, both weakest at lowest complexity — refuting the repeat-artefact reading rather than merely surviving it; and coverage/mappability, where the model's tail holds under coverage-matched negatives (5.280 → 5.096).

06 — LimitsWhat this does not establish

open

The calibrator is from a different study than the editor. A batch difference between studies could in principle blunt the editor arm. A within-study rerun against a same-study deaminase-free control is the outstanding check — it is the last live alternative explanation for the negative, and the one result here that could still move. Everything else on this page has been through its confound.

by design

Enrichment is the endpoint, not AUROC. Every ratio above sits beside its own random baseline; a base rate of 1/11 makes 11.000× the arithmetic ceiling, so a value near it would mean leakage rather than success. The deaminase-free control's ~1.0–1.29× is the floor a real editor signal has to clear, not 1.0.

provenance

The training positives are APOBEC3-family and YTCA-skewed, not verifiably A3A. The A3A-versus-A3B dichotomy did not validate, so "cancer-trained APOBEC model" is the honest description of what was transferred.

Noise floor

The built-in random baseline should read exactly 1.000×. Across 18 blocks it is 0.973 ± 0.062. Differences below ~12% at top 0.1% are not resolvable — which covers the 5.036 vs 5.280 gap, and retired an earlier claim that one model “beat” another when it only matched it.

Blocked

Tier A/B stratified recall — the spec's headline metric — needs COSMIC Tier 1, ClinGen HI Level 3 and DepMap common essentials. None are on either node. That's a data acquisition task, not an analysis one; only the coding/intergenic split could be built.

Prior

Calibration is to the sampled 1-in-11 base rate, which is an artefact of negative sampling and not genome-wide prevalence. Any deployed threshold must be re-based before a risk number is quoted to anyone.

Cohort

The training set rests on 21 donors, not the 133 available at lower purity. Purity beat volume on the tail, but it cost donor diversity, and that bounds how far these numbers generalise.

Not run

Fine-tuning the foundation model was pre-registered as unwarranted if frozen embeddings failed to beat the baseline. They scored 1.048×. Deliberately not attempted, rather than attempted and quietly dropped.
